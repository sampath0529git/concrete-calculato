How much concrete do I need?

Concrete is ordered by volume in cubic metres (m³). For a flat slab, shed base or path the sum is simple: length × width × thickness, with every measurement in metres. A 3m × 3m base poured 100mm (0.1m) deep needs 0.9 m³. Add a waste allowance of 5–10% because ground is never perfectly level and formwork flexes.

How to calculate cubic metres of concrete

  1. Measure length and width in metres.
  2. Convert thickness from millimetres to metres (100mm = 0.1m).
  3. Multiply the three numbers to get the volume.
  4. Multiply by 1.05–1.10 to allow for waste.

For a post hole or column

Use π × (diameter ÷ 2)² × depth. A 0.3m wide, 0.6m deep fence post hole needs about 0.042 m³, roughly four bags of 25kg mix, less the space the post takes up.

Concrete calculator FAQs

How do I calculate cubic metres of concrete?

Multiply length × width × thickness, all in metres. A 3m × 3m slab at 100mm thick is 3 × 3 × 0.1 = 0.9 m³. Add 5–10% for waste.

How many bags of concrete make one cubic metre?

A 25kg bag makes about 0.012 m³, so one cubic metre needs around 83 bags. A 20kg bag makes about 0.0095 m³ (around 105 bags).

How much concrete do I need for a shed base?

A 2.4m × 1.8m base at 100mm thick is 0.43 m³, or about 0.47 m³ with 10% waste. That is roughly 40 bags of 25kg mix.

Is it cheaper to buy bags or ready-mix?

Below about 0.5 m³ bags are usually simpler and cheaper. Above 1 m³ ready-mix is normally cheaper and far less work, though small loads can attract a short-load charge.

How thick should a concrete slab be?

Paths and patios 75–100mm, shed bases 100mm, car driveways 100–150mm. Check Building Regulations for structural work.

Why add waste to my calculation?

Uneven ground, formwork movement and spillage mean you use more than the theoretical volume. A 10% allowance avoids running short mid-pour.
